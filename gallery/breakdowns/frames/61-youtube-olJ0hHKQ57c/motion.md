# Motion notes: How 3 Students Built a €130K ARR AI Learning System

Lovable · 02:21.60 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=olJ0hHKQ57c

The movement is almost all typographic. Most spoken lines appear on screen word by word as they are said, placed in the space beside the speaker, sized up for numbers and switched to an italic serif for the word that carries feeling. Between the interviews, the graphics live on one black paper surface: polaroids stack inside parentheses, archive images gather around a date, documents slide up with a highlighted line, and pictures take the place of words in a sentence. The sequences below sample those moves, the stop-motion light shapes, the lantern-slide maps and the closing title.

## How to read this

Each sequence is a run of frames sampled at a fixed rate, listed in order with its timestamp, so stepping through the files is a flip book of the move. The bar after each frame is the motion energy from it to the next frame: the mean change in RGB between consecutive source frames (24 fps, downscaled to 96x54), on a log scale from the film's noise floor to its 99th percentile. ▁ is a hold, █ is a full-frame wipe or the fastest movement in the film, and a small shape gliding across a flat field sits around ▃–▅. A run like ▁▂▅█▅▂▁ is an ease-in-out; █▅▃▂▁ is an ease-out that lands and settles. `trail.webp`, where present, is an onion skin of the sequence: moving shapes from every frame over the static background, earlier positions fainter.

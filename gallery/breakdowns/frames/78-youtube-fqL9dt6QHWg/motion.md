# Motion notes: 5 times scientists experimented on themselves - Shannon Odell

TED-Ed · 06:15.88 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fqL9dt6QHWg

Caitlin McCarthy draws the film in a short list of colors, ultramarine and navy, lemon, coral, pink, mint and one green, with grain on every gradient and a soft glow along the outlines, so the whole film looks lit from behind like a screen. Characters are flat and rubbery, and camera moves step forward every eighth of a second, three frames at a time, which is why the cut detector counts 222 cuts. Many of them are the steps of one pan or push. Most scene changes grow out of something already in the frame: a studio backdrop pulled away, a heart-shaped iris that cracks, a coin that flips from one scientist to the next, a hazard sign whose silhouette fills in, a tie that opens into a stomach, a cell that turns out to be on a mouse. Repetition is shown by multiplying the frame (diners, arms, mouths), and time by a window that flips between sun and moon. The sequences below follow each story's key moves in order.

## How to read this

Each sequence is a run of frames sampled at a fixed rate, listed in order with its timestamp, so stepping through the files is a flip book of the move. The bar after each frame is the motion energy from it to the next frame: the mean change in RGB between consecutive source frames (24 fps, downscaled to 96x54), on a log scale from the film's noise floor to its 99th percentile. ▁ is a hold, █ is a full-frame wipe or the fastest movement in the film, and a small shape gliding across a flat field sits around ▃–▅. A run like ▁▂▅█▅▂▁ is an ease-in-out; █▅▃▂▁ is an ease-out that lands and settles. `trail.webp`, where present, is an onion skin of the sequence: moving shapes from every frame over the static background, earlier positions fainter.

# Motion notes: A Stanford Student Builds Circuit Boards With Devin Bots

Cognition · 01:02.10 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=plozb8n4M5w

Almost all the movement in this film is in the cutting, not in the camera. Most setups are locked off or barely drift, and a 62-second film has 32 shots across eight positions around one table, plus two graphic sequences. The moves worth studying are the hand-offs: from an outtake to a look down the lens, from the room into a flat window of her own videos and back out into the flattest camera angle, from a sticker star to the real star in her hair, and the interface's steady move into the room, from a full-screen chat to a chip over the footage to name tags placed on the table. The sequences below sample those changes.

## How to read this

Each sequence is a run of frames sampled at a fixed rate, listed in order with its timestamp, so stepping through the files is a flip book of the move. The bar after each frame is the motion energy from it to the next frame: the mean change in RGB between consecutive source frames (24 fps, downscaled to 96x54), on a log scale from the film's noise floor to its 99th percentile. ▁ is a hold, █ is a full-frame wipe or the fastest movement in the film, and a small shape gliding across a flat field sits around ▃–▅. A run like ▁▂▅█▅▂▁ is an ease-in-out; █▅▃▂▁ is an ease-out that lands and settles. `trail.webp`, where present, is an onion skin of the sequence: moving shapes from every frame over the static background, earlier positions fainter.

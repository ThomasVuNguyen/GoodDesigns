The incredible engineering of Venice - Stephanie H. Smith

A TED-Ed lesson on how Venice was built on wooden piles in a lagoon, directed by Sofia Pashaei in flat geometric shapes with no outlines. Almost every scene change is a circle that grows from one small detail of the frame (a pile, a door, a coin) and opens on the next scene. The breakdown samples 30 moves as flip books, from piles driven by giant fingers to a flood that rises and falls against San Marco.

Motion

Sofia Pashaei draws Venice in flat geometric shapes with no outlines: stacked bands of blue for water, gradient skies, and buildings built from rectangles and arches. Almost every scene change is the same move. A small dot appears on one detail of the current picture, grows into a solid circle that fills the frame, and the next scene is waiting inside it. Within scenes the motion is mostly mechanical and repeated: piles driven one after another, boats sliding past on loops, tiles stacking in a grid, a route drawing itself across a map.

Each sequence below is sampled at a fixed rate so its frames read as a flip book. Bars show how much the picture changes from one source frame to the next: tall bars are fast moves, flat stretches are holds. The same notes are in motion.md for reading as text.

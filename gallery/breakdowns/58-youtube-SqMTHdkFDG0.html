Motion

MoMA's trailer for its YouTube channel is a montage of clips from its series, about 55 cuts in 68 seconds, framed by outtakes: it opens on a slate and color bars and closes on 'You want me to clap?'. The cutting runs in bursts. Half-second inserts of hands at work alternate with longer held beats for a joke or a reaction, and archive film, animation and camcorder video are cut straight into the cinematic interviews. The only graphics are two centered lines in bold white sans and the MoMA wordmark, each appearing on a cut rather than animating in.

Each sequence below is sampled at a fixed rate so its frames read as a flip book. Bars show how much the picture changes from one source frame to the next: tall bars are fast moves, flat stretches are holds. The same notes are in motion.md for reading as text.

  1. 01An outtake as the opening

    • outtake
    • color bars
    • slate
    • hard cut

    A man at his editing desk asks 'What's the tone of this?', turns away and rubs his head. At 2.9 a single frame of color bars cuts in, then a slate claps in front of him, and at 3.9 the film jumps to a deep wide of a studio. The tape-and-slate leader tells you this is the people behind the work, before any art appears.

    25 frames, 6 per second (0.167 s apart)

  2. 02The half-second montage

    • fast cutting
    • on the beat
    • inserts

    Eight shots in about four seconds, each roughly half a second: a conservator's tools, a microscope over a textile, a macro of burlap threads, a negative held to the light, a technician reaching up to a shelf, handlers on a hoist, a painting on a rack, a team at a glass table. Every shot is mid-gesture, so the cuts read as one continuous motion of hands.

    36 frames, 8 per second (0.125 s apart)

  3. 03The crate that won't fit

    • comic beat
    • locked-off
    • held shot

    After the montage, a held shot of an open freight elevator. A huge white crate lid tilts out over the doorway and a man in a yellow shirt ducks under it and pushes it back up. The trailer gives slapstick four full seconds, about eight times what its neighbors get.

    17 frames, 4 per second (0.250 s apart)

  4. 04Shot and reverse down a cone

    • reverse angle
    • cut on action
    • wide lens

    An older woman blows into a long metal cone, seen from her end. Hard cut to the other end on a wide lens: the cone's open mouth fills the foreground and she is small at the far end of the room. The cut flips the depth of the frame.

    11 frames, 6 per second (0.167 s apart)

  5. 05The Red Studio pulls back into a collage

    • collage
    • pull-back
    • pop-on
    • stagger

    Matisse's Red Studio fills the frame, then snaps back in a couple of frames to a small card on pale blue. The works it pictures pop on around it one after another, about one every eighth of a second, with no scale or fade, until the card is ringed by them.

    11 frames, 8 per second (0.125 s apart)

  6. 06A ring of tulips turns

    • hand-drawn
    • rotation
    • boiling line

    Hand-drawn animation of a black eel inside an oval of tulips. The whole ring turns and the eel swims round with it, while every stem is redrawn each frame, so the line wobbles as it moves.

    12 frames, 8 per second (0.125 s apart)

  7. 07From a silent stare to a scream

    • hard cut
    • whip
    • camcorder
    • contrast

    A still, sepia silent-film face inside an iris vignette, eyes wide. Hard cut to handheld camcorder video on a crowded street: the camera whips round and a man's face swings into the lens, screaming, with low-resolution color and smeared motion. Two archive formats are cut back to back for contrast.

    23 frames, 12 per second (0.083 s apart)

  8. 08A lift across the white gallery

    • dance
    • locked-off
    • white field

    Locked-off on an empty white gallery. A group walks toward the camera and two dancers raise a woman overhead, arms spread, as they come forward. On the white field the onion skin shows the rise as one stack of poses.

    9 frames, 4 per second (0.250 s apart)

  9. 09Two lines of type, each on its own shot

    • type
    • hard on
    • centered

    'Passionate perspectives on art' appears in bold white sans with the cut to a photographer beside a bronze head, and holds without moving. On the next cut, to an extreme close-up through a magnifier, it is replaced by 'Subscribe on YouTube' in the same place. The type changes only when the shot does.

    14 frames, 4 per second (0.250 s apart)

  10. 10A clap, then the wordmark

    • gesture
    • logo
    • hard on

    The last interviewee claps her hands, and as they come together the white MoMA wordmark appears over her. She holds the folded-hands pose under it to the end.

    9 frames, 8 per second (0.125 s apart)

# Video frame and transcript study

The local `index.html` previews the current GoodDesigns gallery with a **Breakdown** button on videos 1–13, 16, 17, 28–35, and 41–78. Other video cards have no breakdown button. The individual breakdown pages show video playback beside a clickable transcript and a frame library with scene-change and one-second views. Hovering over a frame shows a larger preview and its **Play frame** control; keyboard focus shows the preview too. On touch screens, the play control remains visible.

For local review, serve the repository root with `npx -y http-server . -p 8766 -a 127.0.0.1 -c-1` and open `http://127.0.0.1:8766/notes/good-videos-video-study/index.html#videos`. This server supports video byte ranges, so frame timestamps seek reliably.

The published copy lives in `gallery/breakdowns/`. Run `python3 scripts/publish-video-breakdowns.py` after rebuilding the local study to refresh its pages and WebP frames. Short videos have compact 540p playable copies; videos 29–33 use the YouTube player to avoid hosting hours of video. Their frame and transcript buttons still seek the player. The new long-video frames are 720 pixels wide; earlier frames are 960 pixels wide. CI copies these committed gallery files into the live site; the full-resolution study media remains local and ignored by Git.

Each video page has playback, a timestamped transcript, one frame per second, and extra frames at detected scene cuts. The local study also has a ten-second visual overview. The one-second source frames are 1280 pixels wide at most. Scene cuts use FFmpeg's scene score threshold of 0.30. This selection is intended for visual and pacing analysis; it does not represent every decoded video frame.

Video 34, Apple's MacBook Pro M6 film, opens with 20 handpicked frames from the one-second sequence instead of detected cuts. Its moving masks and typography rarely trigger the cut detector, so the automatic set misses most of the visual ideas. The exhaustive every-second view remains available in the second tab. The curated timestamps live in `build_study.py` and the generated `manifest.json`.

Video 35, Google Labs’ Playground launch film, opens with 30 handpicked shots, each with a note on camera position, lens and movement. The film reuses a few locked-off frames and swaps the room into different games inside them, so the notes call out those match frames. The notes live in `SHOT_NOTES` in `build_study.py`.

Videos 41–46 are Kevin Ngo's animations drawn frame by frame in JavaScript by Claude: “What do you love?”, the fruit fly and honey bee life cycles, an untitled riso-print sound piece, the Sonnet 5 versus Sonnet 5.5 dinosaur split screen, and “Thanks @claudeai”. Each opens on handpicked **Key frames** with a note on style, composition or transition, also kept in `SHOT_NOTES`. Their soundtracks are music only. Whisper produced nothing but hallucinated lines, so their pages say there is no narration instead of showing a transcript. Most are square, so their frame grids use square cards. The dinosaur film has no hard cuts, so its scene-cut list is empty.

Video 47 is only the first eight seconds of Kimi AI’s “Build 3D Websites with Kimi Code – Tutorial #4”, the cold open. The rest of the video is not kept because its music never ducks under the narration. The clip is sampled every quarter second (`FRAME_STEP` in `build_study.py`), so its frame library has an **Every quarter second** tab, and its 14 **Beats** notes sit in `SHOT_NOTES`. Its transcript is the YouTube auto captions trimmed to the clip, with “Rachel” corrected to the on-screen “Raquel”. In the gallery, the entry’s `end: 8` stops the YouTube player at eight seconds.

Video 48 is Google’s “Detection and Response”, episode two of HACKING GOOGLE. It runs 16 minutes, so its page uses the YouTube player like videos 29–33, and its 36 **Key frames** trace the film’s visual system: interviews on an LED stage that plays a white voxel world, the fake-researcher montage, and the fire metaphor carried through to the end. Its transcript is the creator captions, with each multi-line cue joined into one line.

Videos 49–51 are Notion customer films: La Cabra’s Chelsea opening, La Cabra’s operations across cities, and Monumental Labs’ proposal agent. They have no creator captions, so their transcripts come from local Whisper (`mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo`). Whisper looped hallucinated lines over the music outros of 49 and 50, and those cues were removed. In 49, “La Capra” and “Espen” were corrected to the on-screen “La Cabra” and “Esben”.

Video 52 is Google Gemini's “Keep Projects Moving in the Background with Tasks in Gemini”, and video 53 is TED-Ed's “4 ways to tell a great story”. Both have creator captions and **Key frames** notes in `SHOT_NOTES`. They are the first pages with a **Motion** section, built by `motion_sequences.py`, for films whose movement does not survive one frame per second. The TED-Ed film is animated by Kozmonot Animation Studio in flat dots on flat color and has one hard cut in five minutes; every other change is a dive through a dot, a bubble or a letter, an iris, or a fire-swirl wipe. Its page samples 41 moves as flip books, each at its own rate (1.5 to 12 frames per second), with a note on what moves and how. Gemini's page has four, mostly its type and logo end card.

The Motion section has three parts. A motion-energy timeline across the film is the mean RGB change between consecutive source frames at 96×54, on a log scale from the film's noise floor to its 99th percentile, so a small dot crossing a flat field still registers next to full-frame wipes. Each sequence has a frame strip and a per-step energy bar chart. Sequences with `trail: True` also get an onion-skin image (moving pixels from each frame over the median background, older frames fainter), which only reads well where the background holds still. The same content is written as text to `frames/<video>/motion.md`, with frame paths, timestamps and energy as block characters, so an agent reading the folder can follow the motion in order. The frames sit in `frames/<video>/motion/<sequence>/`, and the per-frame energy is cached in `energy.json` beside them.

Video 54 is TED-Ed's “What is sumo, and why is it so popular?”, animated by Masayoshi Nakamura and Good General. Unlike video 53 it is cut conventionally (129 detected cuts), and most of its motion is character animation, so its 41 sequences are walk and run cycles, ritual poses, throws and falls, plus the dives and tunnels between scenes. Onion skins are kept only for the six where the background holds still.

Video 55 is TED-Ed's “The incredible engineering of Venice”, directed by Sofia Pashaei. Nearly every scene change is a circle that grows from one detail into the next scene, so its 30 sequences mostly start or end on one. Its source needed a retry: YouTube refused the audio stream once, and the first file came down silent.

Video 56 is TED-Ed's “Do imaginary friends make you smarter?”, directed by Caitlin McCarthy. It is full character animation, and its 35 sequences follow Zelba's shapeshifting and the soft-edged color blooms that carry each scene change.

Video 57 is MoMA's “How to Make an Artist Book”, a 27-minute In the Studio demonstration by Rhea Karam. It is the first long live-action film with a **Motion** section, so its page uses the YouTube player and 720-pixel frames like video 48. Its 69 **Key frames** cover the eight chapter titles, the reference cards for other artists' books and her own, and each step from sketch to cover. Its 16 sequences are mostly hand gestures (taping, scoring, peeling and pressing the binding, the guillotine cut) and the title reveals. The timeline labels every two minutes on films over ten minutes. The installed yt-dlp got 403 errors from YouTube; a current release downloaded it, with the original English audio picked from the dubbed tracks.

Video 58 is MoMA's 68-second trailer for its YouTube channel, a montage of about 55 cuts framed by on-set outtakes. Its 43 **Key frames** cover the montage shot by shot, and its 10 sequences cover the opening slate, the half-second inserts, the Red Studio collage, the cut from a silent-film stare to a camcorder scream, and the type and wordmark that appear on cuts. Several sequences end just before a cut so their frames and onion skins stay inside one shot.

Videos 59–68 add a **Craft** section, written so people and agents can learn how a film is put together, not only what is in it. They are three Lovable founder stories (WeGlow, Saath, Klar), Lovable's "Ideas are everywhere" and "Some Ideas Are Too Loud to Ignore", Notion's "Think Together", "Meet Custom Agents" and "History of Search", Fireship's Nvidia story and SpaceX's 21-minute "Starship - Go For Orbit". The section opens with a lede, then a film map: four lanes (story, voices, sound, picture) laid against the running time, where every segment plays the film from that point and a playhead follows playback. Below it are short essays on only the crafts each film actually uses (story, voices and script, how the frames work together, the world and its graphics, showing the product, music and sound, trust and warmth), each pinned to timestamped frames. Then come reusable techniques and a line on what the film does not lean on. An aspect is left out when the film has nothing worth learning there, so SpaceX has no graphics section and its sound section covers only where the score drops out. The same notes are written to `frames/<video>/craft.md`. `craft.py` renders the section and lists the allowed lane kinds and aspect ids.

From video 59 on, each film's notes live in one file, `studies/<NN>-<slug>.json`: the gallery note, key-frame notes, Motion sequences, the craft notes and transcript settings, loaded by `build_study.py`. Every film here has a Motion section, but its sequences are only the moves worth studying, so SpaceX's five cover camera moves and cuts that establish scale. Their transcripts are local Whisper (`mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo`, run with `uvx --from mlx-whisper`), corrected against on-screen text and a second pass on the isolated voice. YouTube rate-limited caption downloads. Films 64–66 carry no transcript: 64 and 65 have no speech, and 66 is a song whose lyrics could not be heard reliably. The sound notes rest on evidence. Demucs (`htdemucs`, two stems, run on CPU because MPS fails) splits each soundtrack into voice and everything else, and `prep_analysis.py <NN>` writes an ignored `analysis/<NN>/` kit: contact sheets, a shot list, stem loudness every half second, spectrograms, and a tempo estimate checked against the cuts. Sections claim cuts land on the beat only when that check beats chance, and none of these films did.

Videos 69 and 70 follow the same pattern. Notion's "Introducing Notion Faces" has no speech and no change of background, so the cut detector finds none of its four instant changes. Helios's launch film, from an X post, is narrated by its two founders. Their supers name them, and the voice stem was matched against their on-camera stretches to attribute the lines heard over other footage.

Video 71 is HubSpot's 17-minute "Stories of Scale" episode on go-to-market engineering at Clay, a day-in-the-life vlog with an explainer built in. Like the other films over ten minutes, its page uses the YouTube player and 720-pixel frames. Its 22 Motion sequences are mostly the green explainer board and the timeline and counter graphics, and its Craft section follows how the vlog footage, the studio set and the drawn explanations take turns. The installed yt-dlp was out of date, so a current release ran through `uvx`. Demucs needed `--with numpy` added to its `uvx` environment. Its transcript drops a run of hallucinated "I have water" lines at the very end.

Videos 72–74 are three spots from The Browser Company: "Arc on Windows. Download now.", "Tidy Tabs by Arc" and "Introducing Arc for iPhone". They are 37, 21 and 41 seconds long, so they get compact playable copies. Arc on Windows opens on a recorded voice reading Marshall McLuhan's lines from *The Medium Is the Massage*. Whisper heard "allatonceness" as "all experiences", so the transcript follows the book. The film does not credit the recording, so the notes credit the words, not the voice. Whisper also heard "Bingo" and "Venga" in the music; both were dropped. Tidy Tabs has no speech, so it carries no transcript. Arc for iPhone keeps only its two spoken lines. Its song's lyrics are left out, along with a run of hallucinated "Where's my phone?" cues. The current yt-dlp needed `--js-runtimes node --remote-components ejs:github` to get full-quality formats.

Video 75 is Anthropic's 72-second brand film "What we make of this moment, will make this moment." Its script is almost all on-screen type: thirteen questions spelled a word or two per shot, then 30 project names in monospace, then the title line with 23 customer logos flicked through its "we" in 2.2 seconds. The project labels and logos were read from full-resolution crops at every frame, with Tesseract as a check that no short label was missed. The one spoken line, under the opening logo, is uncredited; Whisper repeated it twice over the music, and those copies were dropped. The 19-second project dive pushes and dissolves from one image into the next, so the cut detector finds only two cuts in it.

Video 76 is a 30-second Dia spot from The Browser Company, posted on X by Josh Miller. Nobody speaks, so it has no transcript; Whisper returned only hallucinated "Thank you." lines, and the isolated voice stem is empty apart from one bird's coo, which also shows the on-screen screams are silent. Because the breakdown is mostly about sound, its sound notes also rest on an AudioSet sound-event tagger (`MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593` through `transformers`, run with `uvx`) applied in 0.75-second windows every quarter second, and on mix loudness every quarter second. The tagger's labels (ding, plop, siren, bang, birdsong, coo, whoosh) were checked against the picture before use.

Video 77 is Jon Bois's “Why you should never vote: an explainer”, a 2:37 parody posted on May 4, 2016. It has no YouTube captions, so its transcript is local Whisper on the Demucs voice stem, checked against a second pass on the full mix. “Q83” was corrected to the on-screen “Q-83”, and one sentence that Whisper split across two cues was joined. Both passes hear “1,000 Americans” at 2:00, but the chart on screen reads “10,000”, so the transcript keeps what is heard and its note mentions the difference. Nearly all of the film is one mint canvas crossed by a virtual camera, and the cut detector finds only 11 cuts, the first at 0:46.7. Its 40 Key frames and 14 Motion sequences were picked from frame strips sampled every quarter to half second: the camera's tilts, whips, spins and pull-backs, the night-vision corridor, the inverted voter footage, the pie chart and the closing phone footage of a laptop. The music has a steady pulse of about 129 BPM, but only 3 of the 11 cuts land on a beat, which is what chance would give, so the notes make no beat claim.

Video 78 is TED-Ed's “5 times scientists experimented on themselves”, a 6-minute lesson by Shannon Odell, directed by Caitlin McCarthy (who also directed video 56) and narrated by Addison Anderson; the score and sound are by cAMP Studio, read from the end card. Its transcript is the creator captions with each multi-line cue joined into one line. Its camera moves advance in steps about every three frames, so many of the cut detector's 222 cuts are steps of a pan or push, and the Craft section describes the stepping instead of quoting a cut rate. Only the X-ray stairs sequence keeps an onion-skin trail; the cheering ward and the sickbed time-lapse turned to mud. 84 of the 222 cuts land within 70 ms of a beat against about 79 by chance, so the notes make no beat claim. Demucs puts most of the closing sting into the voice stem, but Whisper finds no words there. It has 98 Key frames and 41 Motion sequences, and at under ten minutes it gets a compact playable copy.

Only videos 48–78 still have local sources. `python3 notes/good-videos-video-study/build_study.py 52 53` rebuilds just those videos and keeps the other manifest entries, and `python3 scripts/publish-video-breakdowns.py 52 53` publishes just those pages, frames and `motion.md` files.

`source/` contains the downloaded clips. `frames/` contains the image sequences and `scene-cuts.csv` files. `transcripts/` contains source WebVTT captions where available and locally recognized speech for the other videos. Video 11 is spoken in Japanese, so its transcript is in Japanese. The `.transcript.txt` files are cleaned, timestamped reading copies. Speech recognition can mishear words and names; the `.vtt` files retain the original timing.

The media and generated pages are local study materials. They are excluded from Git so the GoodDesigns gallery release does not accidentally include a large video archive. To rebuild the study after updating the media or transcripts, run `python3 notes/good-videos-video-study/build_study.py` from the repository root.

The standalone motion reference at `gallery/breakdowns/references/cleo-extinct-animals-intro/` contains the 1:07–1:13 intro from Cleo Abram’s “The Real Reason We Should Revive Extinct Animals.” It includes a six-second 1080p clip with audio, 143 individual 1080p WebP frames, a downloadable frame ZIP, and a frame-by-frame viewer. The full source video stays local under `source/`.

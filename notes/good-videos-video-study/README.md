# Video frame and transcript study

The local `index.html` previews the current GoodDesigns gallery with a **Breakdown** button on videos 1–13, 16, 17, 28–35, and 41–51. Other video cards have no breakdown button. The individual breakdown pages show video playback beside a clickable transcript and a frame library with scene-change and one-second views. Hovering over a frame shows a larger preview and its **Play frame** control; keyboard focus shows the preview too. On touch screens, the play control remains visible.

For local review, serve the repository root with `npx -y http-server . -p 8766 -a 127.0.0.1 -c-1` and open `http://127.0.0.1:8766/notes/good-videos-video-study/index.html#videos`. This server supports video byte ranges, so frame timestamps seek reliably.

The published copy lives in `gallery/breakdowns/`. Run `python3 scripts/publish-video-breakdowns.py` after rebuilding the local study to refresh its pages and WebP frames. Short videos have compact 540p playable copies; videos 29–33 use the YouTube player to avoid hosting hours of video. Their frame and transcript buttons still seek the player. The new long-video frames are 720 pixels wide; earlier frames are 960 pixels wide. CI copies these committed gallery files into the live site; the full-resolution study media remains local and ignored by Git.

Each video page has playback, a timestamped transcript, one frame per second, and extra frames at detected scene cuts. The local study also has a ten-second visual overview. The one-second source frames are 1280 pixels wide at most. Scene cuts use FFmpeg's scene score threshold of 0.30. This selection is intended for visual and pacing analysis; it does not represent every decoded video frame.

Video 34, Apple's MacBook Pro M6 film, opens with 20 handpicked frames from the one-second sequence instead of detected cuts. Its moving masks and typography rarely trigger the cut detector, so the automatic set misses most of the visual ideas. The exhaustive every-second view remains available in the second tab. The curated timestamps live in `build_study.py` and the generated `manifest.json`.

Video 35, Google Labs’ Playground launch film, opens with 30 handpicked shots, each with a note on camera position, lens and movement. The film reuses a few locked-off frames and swaps the room into different games inside them, so the notes call out those match frames. The notes live in `SHOT_NOTES` in `build_study.py`.

Videos 41–46 are Kevin Ngo's animations drawn frame by frame in JavaScript by Claude: “What do you love?”, the fruit fly and honey bee life cycles, an untitled riso-print sound piece, the Sonnet 5 versus Sonnet 5.5 dinosaur split screen, and “Thanks @claudeai”. Each opens on handpicked **Key frames** with a note on style, composition or transition, also kept in `SHOT_NOTES`. Their soundtracks are music only. Whisper produced nothing but hallucinated lines, so their pages say there is no narration instead of showing a transcript. Most are square, so their frame grids use square cards. The dinosaur film has no hard cuts, so its scene-cut list is empty.

Video 47 is only the first eight seconds of Kimi AI’s “Build 3D Websites with Kimi Code – Tutorial #4”, the cold open. The rest of the video is not kept because its music never ducks under the narration. The clip is sampled every quarter second (`FRAME_STEP` in `build_study.py`), so its frame library has an **Every quarter second** tab, and its 14 **Beats** notes sit in `SHOT_NOTES`. Its transcript is the YouTube auto captions trimmed to the clip, with “Rachel” corrected to the on-screen “Raquel”. In the gallery, the entry’s `end: 8` stops the YouTube player at eight seconds.

Video 48 is Google’s “Detection and Response”, episode two of HACKING GOOGLE. It runs 16 minutes, so its page uses the YouTube player like videos 29–33, and its 36 **Key frames** trace the film’s visual system: interviews on an LED stage that plays a white voxel world, the fake-researcher montage, and the fire metaphor carried through to the end. Its transcript is the creator captions, with each multi-line cue joined into one line.

Videos 49–51 are Notion customer films: La Cabra’s Chelsea opening, La Cabra’s operations across cities, and Monumental Labs’ proposal agent. They have no creator captions, so their transcripts come from local Whisper (`mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo`). Whisper looped hallucinated lines over the music outros of 49 and 50, and those cues were removed. In 49, “La Capra” and “Espen” were corrected to the on-screen “La Cabra” and “Esben”.

`source/` contains the downloaded clips. `frames/` contains the image sequences and `scene-cuts.csv` files. `transcripts/` contains source WebVTT captions where available and locally recognized speech for the other videos. Video 11 is spoken in Japanese, so its transcript is in Japanese. The `.transcript.txt` files are cleaned, timestamped reading copies. Speech recognition can mishear words and names; the `.vtt` files retain the original timing.

The media and generated pages are local study materials. They are excluded from Git so the GoodDesigns gallery release does not accidentally include a large video archive. To rebuild the study after updating the media or transcripts, run `python3 notes/good-videos-video-study/build_study.py` from the repository root.

The standalone motion reference at `gallery/breakdowns/references/cleo-extinct-animals-intro/` contains the 1:07–1:13 intro from Cleo Abram’s “The Real Reason We Should Revive Extinct Animals.” It includes a six-second 1080p clip with audio, 143 individual 1080p WebP frames, a downloadable frame ZIP, and a frame-by-frame viewer. The full source video stays local under `source/`.
